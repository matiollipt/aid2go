# Scientific Foundations & CAFA Benchmark

## 1. Biological Motivation & The Function Annotation Challenge

With the exponential growth of high-throughput genomic and metagenomic sequencing, millions of protein sequences are deposited into public databases such as UniProtKB annually. However, biochemical validation of protein function through wet-lab experimental assays (e.g., enzyme kinetics, crystallographic ligand binding, gene knockout assays) is laborious, time-consuming, and expensive. As a consequence, fewer than 1% of known protein sequences have experimentally verified functional annotations.

Computational protein function prediction addresses this bottleneck by predicting functional terms directly from amino acid sequences, predicted 3D structures, and interaction networks.

## 2. The Gene Ontology (GO)

The **Gene Ontology (GO)** is the standard worldwide framework for annotating gene and protein function. GO organizes biological concepts into a structured, controlled vocabulary across three orthogonal **subontologies (aspects)**:

1. **Molecular Function (MF)** (Root: `GO:0003674`):
   - Describes elemental biochemical activities performed by a gene product at the molecular level, such as *catalytic activity*, *adenylate cyclase activity*, or *zinc ion binding*.
2. **Biological Process (BP)** (Root: `GO:0008150`):
   - Describes broad multi-step biological programs or pathways orchestrated by multiple gene products, such as *DNA replication*, *mitochondrial transmembrane transport*, or *cell division*.
3. **Cellular Component (CC)** (Root: `GO:0005575`):
   - Describes the subcellular localization, macromolecular complex, or cellular anatomical structure where a protein resides or executes its function, such as *nucleus*, *cytoplasm*, or *integral component of membrane*.

### The Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) & True-Path Rule

GO terms are organized not as a simple flat taxonomy, but as a **Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)** $\mathcal{G}_{GO} = (\mathcal{V}_{GO}, \mathcal{E}_{GO})$ where:
- Nodes $t \in \mathcal{V}_{GO}$ represent discrete functional terms.
- Directed edges $(u, v) \in \mathcal{E}_{GO}$ represent semantic relationships, predominantly `is_a` (subtype) and `part_of` (component part).

#### The True-Path Rule
The defining axiom of GO semantics is the **True-Path Rule**:
> *If a protein is annotated with a specific GO term $t$, it is implicitly annotated with all ancestral terms $\text{Ancestors}(t)$ up to the ontology root.*

Consequently:
- Leaf and deep terms in the DAG represent fine-grained, highly specific functions.
- Root and shallow terms represent general, coarse categories.
- **Hierarchical Monotonicity**: Any predicted probabilistic score assigned to a parent term must be at least as large as the score assigned to any of its children:
  $$P(\text{parent}) \ge \max_{c \in \text{children}(\text{parent})} P(c)$$

In `aid2go`, this property is guaranteed during inference through topological propagation in `aid2go.infer.postprocess.enforce_true_path_rule`.

## 3. Protein Representations: Sequences, Structures & Networks

Aid2Go integrates multi-scale representations of proteins:

### A) Evolutionary Scale Modeling (ESM-2)
Proteins are represented using sequence embeddings generated by **ESM-2** (*Lin et al., 2023, Science*). ESM-2 is a high-capacity protein language model trained on millions of evolutionary diverse sequences via masked language modeling.
- The default model in Aid2Go is `facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`, which extracts 1280-dimensional representations per residue.
- **Mean-Pooled Embeddings**: Residue vectors are averaged across the sequence length to produce a dense global representation for link prediction and similarity search.
- **HDF5 Storage Architecture**: To prevent operating system file-descriptor limits and disk thrashing from millions of individual `.pt` files, embeddings and per-residue tensors are stored in a chunked, indexed HDF5 dataset `protein_data.h5`.

### B) Residue Contact Graphs (3D Structure)
When available (via AlphaFold DB or experimental PDB files), Aid2Go parses C-alpha coordinates ($C_\alpha$) and constructs an internal spatial contact graph:
$$\mathcal{E}_{contact} = \{(i, j) \mid \|C_\alpha^{(i)} - C_\alpha^{(j)}\|_2 \le 8.0\,\text{\AA}\}$$
When structural coordinates are unavailable, Aid2Go constructs a local sequence proximity chain graph connecting adjacent residues ($|i - j| \le 2$).

### C) FAISS Sequence-Similarity Networks (Data-Driven PPI)
Rather than relying on static, species-specific PPI databases with incomplete coverage, Aid2Go builds a global $k$-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) sequence-similarity network over the ESM-2 protein embedding space using **FAISS** (*Johnson et al., 2019*).
- Embeddings are $L_2$-normalized to compute exact cosine similarity via `IndexFlatIP`.
- The resulting network connects evolutionary and functionally related proteins across diverse taxa.

## 4. The CAFA Competition Framework

The **Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA)** is the gold-standard biennial international challenge (*Radivojac et al., 2013*; *Zhou et al., 2019*) evaluating automated protein function prediction methods under strict blind-testing conditions:

1. **Target Categories**:
   - **No-Knowledge (NK) Targets**: Proteins with zero prior experimental annotations across any GO aspect at the time of submission.
   - **Limited-Knowledge (LK) Targets**: Proteins with annotations in one or two GO aspects, but lacking verified annotations in the target aspect.
2. **Time-Split Evaluation**:
   - Competing models predict functions on unannotated protein targets at Submission Deadline $T_0$.
   - Ground truth is accumulated over a 6 to 12 month accumulation period $T_1$ through newly published experimental papers indexed by UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot (evidence codes: `EXP`, `IDA`, `IPI`, `IMP`, `IGI`, `IEP`, `TAS`, `IC`).
3. **Official Submission Format**:
   - Submissions must be delivered as 3-column tab-separated files without header:
     ```text
     <Protein_ID>    <GO_ID>    <Confidence_Score>
     ```
   - Confidence scores must be floating-point values in the range $(0.000, 1.000]$ formatted to 3 decimal places.
